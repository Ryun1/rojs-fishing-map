      How the ratings and scores work
      • Fishability (Good, Fair, Poor, Unsafe) describes the forecast for the open sea near each spot, reduced for the spot's shelter. It cannot see a rogue set, a wet ledge or surge in a gully. Look at the sea before you go down, and more so at night.
      • Waves: each spot has the wind and swell directions that reach it from open ocean, and those that only cross a channel to Faial or São Jorge. Swell from the open sector counts in full; swell across a channel counts about a third, and swell from behind the island counts for little, because some always wraps around headlands. Wind waves count a little more across a channel.
      • Wind is called onshore when it blows within 60 degrees of straight at the spot, offshore when it blows from behind, and cross otherwise. Onshore wind has lower limits.
      • Official IPMA warnings for the central group are checked: an orange or red sea warning makes every spot Unsafe; a yellow sea warning makes spots open to the swell Unsafe and the rest no better than Poor; any wind warning makes every spot no better than Poor.
      • The limits used are listed with each spot.
      • Wind comes from the Open-Meteo forecast over the sea next to each spot. Waves come from the Open-Meteo marine model at a point a few kilometres off the right coast. Both are on grids of about 8 to 9 km, so wind funnelling and shelter behind the mountain are not modelled. Spot positions and facings come from the OpenStreetMap coastline and have not been checked on the ground.
      • Each species is scored from 0 to 100 on season, sea temperature, wave height at the spot, wind, time of day against sunrise and sunset, tide movement and the kind of spot. These are starting estimates, to be corrected with local knowledge and catch logs.
      • Species pictures are from your own catches, except the moray, which is a drawing. Moon phase is shown for planning and feeds the best-times timeline, but does not change the species scores.
      • Tide is computed on the phone for Horta from published tidal constants, for any date and with no connection, and matches the official Instituto Hidrográfico table to within a few minutes and about 5 cm. It is the astronomical tide only: wind and air pressure can move the real sea level. All five spots use Horta's times. Tidal constants: TICON-4 (Hart-Davis, Dettmering and Seitz, CC BY 4.0).
      • Sources: Open-Meteo (wind and waves), IPMA (warnings), map © OpenStreetMap contributors.
