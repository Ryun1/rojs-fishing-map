          How the ratings and scores work

          • Fishability (Good, Fair, Poor, Unsafe) describes the forecast for the open sea near each spot, reduced for the spot's shelter. It cannot see a rogue set, a wet ledge or surge in a gully. Look at the sea before you go down, and more so at night.
          • Waves: each spot has the wind and swell directions that reach it from open ocean, and those that only cross a channel to Faial or São Jorge. Swell from the open sector counts in full; swell across a channel counts about a third, and swell from behind the island counts for little, because some always wraps around headlands. Wind waves count a little more across a channel.
          • Wind is called onshore when it blows within 60 degrees of straight at the spot, offshore when it blows from behind, and cross otherwise. Onshore wind has lower limits. Wind is shown on the Beaufort scale (force 0 to 12); the forecast comes in km/h and is converted.
          • Official IPMA warnings for the central group are checked: an orange or red sea warning makes every spot Unsafe; a yellow sea warning makes spots open to the swell Unsafe and the rest no better than Poor; any wind warning makes every spot no better than Poor.
          • The limits used are listed with each spot.
          • Wind comes from the Open-Meteo forecast over the sea next to each spot. Waves come from the Open-Meteo marine model at a point a few kilometres off the right coast. Both are on grids of about 8 to 9 km, so wind funnelling and shelter behind the mountain are not modelled. Spot positions and facings come from the OpenStreetMap coastline and have not been checked on the ground.
          • Each species gets a score from 0 to 100: how often it is caught from the shore, times how much it is in season, times how active it is at this time of day, times how well this spot suits it, times a small adjustment for the conditions. Because the parts are multiplied, a fish that is rarely caught from the shore, or out of season, or asleep, cannot score high however good the rest is. Each part is labelled. Measured: the season curves, from 32 years of monthly fish-auction landings on Pico (SREA, 1994 to 2025; commercial boat catches, so a rough guide to when each fish is around rather than when it bites from the shore), and the time of day for sargo, veja, bicuda and moray, from tracking studies. Estimated: how often each fish is caught from the shore, how well each spot suits it, the time of day for the others, and whether warmer or cooler water than normal for the date helps; the sea only counts when it is at least 1 °C off its 1991 to 2020 average. Angler lore: swell and tide, which can move a score by a few points at most. Wind is left to the fishability rating.
          • Colours: green, amber and red always mean fishability (Good, Fair, Poor, Unsafe). How likely a fish is uses teal, worded Likely (70 and up), Possible (45 to 69) and Unlikely. Tap a fish to see what went into its score.
          • This week shows the worst fishability in each part of each day: morning 06:00 to 12:00, afternoon 12:00 to 18:00 and evening 18:00 to 22:00. Tap a box to look at that time and spot.
          • With no forecast for the chosen time, spots are not rated: the app never fills the gap with made-up values. Tide, light and the fish in season are still shown, and the fish are ranked by season alone.
          • Species pictures are from your own catches, except the moray, which is a drawing. Moon phase is shown for planning only; it does not change any rating or score.
          • Tide is computed on the phone for Horta from published tidal constants, for any date and with no connection, and matches the official Instituto Hidrográfico table to within a few minutes and about 5 cm. It is the astronomical tide only: wind and air pressure can move the real sea level. All five spots use Horta's times. Tidal constants: TICON-4 (Hart-Davis, Dettmering and Seitz, CC BY 4.0).
